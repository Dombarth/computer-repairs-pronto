RTX 5090 12V-2x6 Connector and the Melting Reports

The RTX 5090 is rated at 575W and takes its power through a single 12V-2x6 connector, melted connectors and cables were reported from February 2025, and a damaged socket on the card can usually be replaced from $370. What was reported, and by whom:

ReportWhat was found
A Reddit owner, February 2025 (HotHardware)A Founders Edition run with a third-party cable showed burn damage at both ends of the cable, on the power supply and on the card.
Der8auer, February 2025 (Tom's Hardware)On his own Founders Edition at about 570W, one of six 12V wires carried over 22A against a 9.5A per-pin rating, and the power-supply end passed 150°C within four minutes.
Buildzoid's analysis (HotHardware)The RTX 3090 Ti spread its six 12V wires across three shunt resistors to balance the load. The RTX 4090 and 5090 Founders Edition use one, so the card cannot see each wire's current.
Later reports (PCWorld, October 2025)More burnt connectors, including MSI's yellow-tipped plugs, one on an MSI RTX 5090 Gaming Trio.
NVIDIABefore launch, an NVIDIA representative said such issues were expected not to occur on RTX 50 cards. After the February reports, NVIDIA declined to comment to TechRadar.

The causes are still argued over: cable quality, plugs not fully seated, worn connectors and the lack of per-wire balancing have all been put forward. According to Corsair, 12V-2x6 changed only the socket, with sense pins 1.5mm shorter and power terminals 0.25mm longer; the cable is unchanged, so an old, worn 12VHPWR cable still fits a new card.

The connector repair:

  1. Plug and socket are inspected under magnification, and the 12V input measured for shorts before any power is applied.
  2. The damaged socket is removed and the pads and traces beneath it checked for heat damage.
  3. A new 12V-2x6 socket is fitted, and the input filtering and power stages behind it are checked.
  4. The card runs near full power while the new connector is watched with a thermal camera.

Your cable: do not reuse a cable that has melted at either end, and check its power-supply end too. Use the adapter supplied with the card or a cable from your power supply's maker, push the plug fully home with the latch engaged, and avoid tight bends at the plug.

RTX 5090 Founders Edition: Three Boards and Liquid Metal

The Founders Edition RTX 5090 is built from three circuit boards joined by flexible cables, with liquid metal between the GPU and the cooler, which changes how it is opened, diagnosed and repaired.

PartWhat it isWhat it means for a repair
Main boardThe GB202 GPU, sixteen GDDR7 chips, the power stages and the angled 12V-2x6 socket. TechPowerUp counted 19 phases for the GPU and 8 for the memory.Almost all component work happens here, and the whole cooler comes off to reach it.
PCIe boardA small separate board with the PCIe 5.0 x16 contacts, linked by a flexible printed cableA card that is not detected may have a fault here or in the cable.
Display boardThe four outputs on a breakout board at the bracket, with its own flexible cableA dead output can be the port, the board or the cable.
Liquid metalOn the GPU, inside a gasket NVIDIA says is there to stop leaks damaging the boardIt must be kept off the board during cleaning; the gasket is checked on every rebuild.

Tom's Hardware's review said that because of the liquid metal the card "should not be disassembled". Do not open one at home first.

The flexible cables are fragile. In October 2025 Notebookcheck reported that a US repair shop, Northridge Fix, found a bent pin and a broken pin on the connector between boards of a Founders Edition that gave no signal after a water block was fitted, and could not source a replacement connector. Damage there is repairable only if the correct part can be found, and you are told after diagnosis, before any repair cost.

Liquid metal or paste? In TechPowerUp's test, reported by Club386, a quality paste ran within about 2°C of the factory liquid metal. You are told beforehand which material goes back on.

RTX 5090 GDDR7 Memory: Layout and Repair

The RTX 5090's 32GB of memory is sixteen 2GB GDDR7 chips around the GPU on a 512-bit bus, and a failed chip can be replaced from $530 when the correct GDDR7 part can be sourced.

  • Layout: TechPowerUp's photos of the Founders Edition board show sixteen Samsung GDDR7 chips around the GPU, each on its own 32-bit slice of the bus, so testing that shows where errors occur points to one chip.
  • No rear memory: those sixteen chips hold all 32GB, unlike the RTX 3090 with memory on both sides.
  • Signalling and error correction: GDDR7 runs here at 28 Gbps per pin using PAM3, three signal levels, and the JEDEC standard adds error correction inside each chip. Bench testing therefore runs hot and under sustained load, not just a quick pass at idle.

Part availability decides a memory repair. GDDR7 is a newer memory type, and the exact chip a card needs is not always available. We do not promise stock. After diagnosis you are told whether the chip can be sourced and roughly how long it will take; if not, you pay the $150 diagnostic and any return postage, nothing more.

When the part is available, all sixteen chips are retested hot after fitting and the memory pads renewed.

Missing ROPs: An RTX 5090 Fault No Repair Can Fix

Some early RTX 5090s shipped with 168 ROPs instead of 176; NVIDIA called it a rare production anomaly, and the remedy is a replacement through the board manufacturer, not a repair.

  • How it came to light: in February 2025 TechPowerUp found 168 active ROPs on a Zotac RTX 5090 Solid, and cards from other makers followed (reported by Igor's Lab).
  • What NVIDIA said: fewer than 0.5% of RTX 5090 and 5090 D cards were affected, along with some 5070 Ti and, in an updated statement, RTX 5080 cards. NVIDIA put the average graphics performance loss at 4%, said AI and compute work is unaffected, told owners to contact their board manufacturer for a replacement, and said the production anomaly had been corrected.
  • How to check: open GPU-Z and read the ROPs figure. A full RTX 5090 shows 176.
  • Why we cannot fix it: ROPs are units inside the GB202 chip itself. No component, firmware or soldering work can restore them.

If a card sent to us shows 168 ROPs, we tell you straight away. For a second-hand card, give the maker the serial number and ask.

RTX 5090 Black Screen and No Display

A black screen on an RTX 5090 is not always a hardware fault: RTX 50 series cards had driver-related black screens in early 2025, so rule out the driver and firmware before sending the card.

  • Launch-period black screens: Tom's Hardware reported black screens on RTX 50 cards from launch, with some owners lowering the PCIe setting or running at 60Hz to work around them. Driver 572.60, in February 2025, listed fixes for "various black screen issues", and NVIDIA said an optional firmware (vBIOS) update carried the same fix.
  • Cards not detected after a driver install: also in February 2025, some RTX 5090 owners reported cards no longer recognised after a driver update, and NVIDIA said it was "investigating the reported issues with the RTX 50 series" (Igor's Lab, citing PC Gamer). If yours is under warranty, it belongs with the retailer.

Before sending the card, reinstall the driver cleanly with Display Driver Uninstaller, check for a firmware update, try another cable and monitor, and reseat the card. If it is still black, detection points the way:

RTX 5090 Artefacting

Artefacts that appear before Windows loads point to the RTX 5090 itself; artefacts in one game only, or straight after a driver change, usually do not. Coloured blocks, lines, sparkles and stretched textures on a hardware fault usually trace to:

  • One of the sixteen GDDR7 chips, often failing only once warm.
  • The memory power supply (eight phases on the Founders Edition).
  • Overheating from a disturbed or degraded thermal interface or tired pads.
  • Physical stress on the board around the GPU, for example from sag or a drop.

Before posting the card, work through the home checks in our GPU artefacting guide.

RTX 5090 Power Stage and Short-Circuit Repair

The RTX 5090's power stages convert 12V into the supplies for the GPU and memory at up to 575W, and when one fails short the card can trip the power supply or stay dark; power-stage repairs start from $430.

Each stage is a DrMOS package, the switching MOSFETs and their driver in one part; the Founders Edition uses stages from Monolithic Power Systems, and partner boards differ, so replacements are matched to the exact board.

  • Finding the short: each rail is measured to ground with the power off, then a small current-limited supply is fed into the shorted rail while a thermal camera shows which part warms.
  • Reading the symptom: a shorted 12V input tends to trip the power supply as soon as the computer starts, while a shorted core or memory rail tends to leave the fans running over a black screen.
  • After the fix: neighbouring stages, the controller and the rail's capacitors are checked, and the first start after repair is on a current-limited supply.

If you suspect a short, stop switching the computer on. On a card drawing this much power, every attempt risks spreading the damage to more parts.

RTX 5090 Shutdowns and Crashes Under Load

If the whole computer switches off under load with an RTX 5090 fitted, check the power supply and the power cable before the card: NVIDIA lists 1000W as the required system power.

  • Power supply: NVIDIA's 1000W figure assumes a Ryzen 9 9950X system, and it recommends a PCIe CEM 5.1 compliant unit. An older or smaller one may shut down at the card's full load.
  • Cabling: the Founders Edition takes 8-pin cables through the adapter in the box, or one 600W PCIe Gen 5 cable. Use separate 8-pin leads on the adapter, not one lead with two plugs.
  • The plug: discolouring, a hot smell or a plug that will not sit flush means stop and see the connector section.
  • Which kind of crash: a game closing points more to memory, heat or drivers; the whole computer switching off points more to power.

A card that passes every bench test costs you the $150 diagnostic, and you get the test results.

How We Diagnose an RTX 5090

An RTX 5090 is diagnosed from the least invasive check to the most, and warranty and manufacturing-defect questions are settled before anything is opened.

  1. History. Purchase date, earlier repairs or water-block fitting, and the cable and power supply the card ran on.
  2. Inspection. The 12V-2x6 socket under magnification, PCIe contacts, outputs and any sign the card has been opened.
  3. Cold measurements. Resistance to ground on the 12V input and every rail, with nothing connected.
  4. Current-limited power-up from a bench supply that cannot feed enough current to cause more damage.
  5. Firmware and ROP count. Where the card starts, firmware is checked against that model and the ROP count read; a card showing 168 goes back with a referral to the manufacturer.
  6. Rails and memory. Each supply checked in start-up order, then all sixteen GDDR7 chips tested hot.
  7. Quote. The fault, a fixed price, any part to be sourced, and whether we think the card is worth it. Work starts only once you approve.

Is an RTX 5090 Worth Repairing Out of Warranty?

Usually yes: an out-of-warranty RTX 5090 is worth far more than a typical board-level repair costs, so repair is normally worth it while the GPU core is healthy. Measure the quote against recently sold listings for working RTX 5090s, not asking prices.

Repair makes sense when:

  • The fault is a connector, a power stage, firmware, a thermal problem or a single memory chip that can be sourced.
  • You would rather keep a card you know than gamble on a used one; replaced parts carry a 12-month warranty for the same fault.

Repair may not make sense when:

  • The card is still under warranty. Go to the retailer first.
  • The GPU core has failed, or burn damage runs through the board.
  • The needed GDDR7 chip or Founders Edition cable cannot be sourced.

As an illustration: if working RTX 5090s are selling for around $4,000 near you, a connector repair from $370, or a connector and power-stage repair together, is a small share of what the card is worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an RTX 5090 be repaired?

Yes, in most cases. Melted 12V-2x6 connectors, power stages, shorts, PCIe damage, firmware and heat faults can be repaired, and a GDDR7 chip replaced when the part can be sourced. A failed GPU core, missing ROPs or burn damage through the board usually cannot be fixed.

Should I send my RTX 5090 to you or back to the retailer?

If the card is still under its manufacturer's warranty, start with the retailer, because under the Australian Consumer Law the consumer guarantees are owed by the business that sold it. Come to us once the warranty has ended, a claim is declined, or for a second-hand card without cover.
