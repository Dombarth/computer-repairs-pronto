Is Your RTX 5080 Still Under Warranty?

If your RTX 5080 is still inside its manufacturer's warranty, take it back to the retailer you bought it from before paying for any repair. The RTX 5080 went on sale on 30 January 2025, so many cards may still be covered.

  • The retailer is your first call. Your consumer guarantee rights under Australian Consumer Law sit with the business that sold you the card.
  • Factory defects need a replacement, not a repair. A GPU with fewer ROPs than specified is one example (see missing ROPs).
  • Check your dates. Each brand sets its own warranty period. Your receipt and the maker's website will tell you where you stand.

A paid repair is the better route when the warranty has run out, a claim has been declined, or the card was bought second-hand and the warranty does not pass to you. Not sure? Ring 02 8428 9224 and we will tell you if the retailer is the right place.

RTX 5080 GDDR7 Memory: Layout and Chip Replacement

The RTX 5080 carries 16GB of GDDR7 as eight 2GB chips on a 256-bit bus, and a failed chip can be replaced from $530 once the correct replacement chip has been sourced.

  • One chip per channel. Each chip owns a 32-bit share of the bus, so errors on one share point to one chip position.
  • Speed. The memory runs at 30 Gbps for 960 GB/s of bandwidth. Hardware Busters found Samsung chips rated for 32 Gbps on a Galax card, running at the 5080's 30 Gbps.
  • More than one maker. Samsung GDDR7 has been found on several RTX 5080s, but NVIDIA sourced the 5080's memory from more than one supplier, so the maker can differ between cards.
  • Supply. In September 2026 Hardware Busters reported that Micron now lists its 2GB GDDR7 chips as end of life, leaving Samsung and SK hynix as the two sources of that density for the RTX 50 series.

Memory repairs depend on getting the correct chip. We match the replacement to the chips already on your card and confirm we can get it before you approve. If it cannot be sourced, we tell you rather than fit a near match.

How a GDDR7 chip is replaced:

  1. Testing at full speed and temperature finds the failing chip position.
  2. The board is preheated, nearby parts shielded, and the chip lifted with controlled hot air.
  3. The solder pads are cleaned and levelled, and the new chip fitted.
  4. All eight chips are retested and the thermal pads renewed at the right thickness.

RTX 5080 Artefacting and Memory Errors

Artefacts on an RTX 5080, such as coloured blocks, lines, flicker or corrupted textures, usually come from a GDDR7 chip, the memory's power supply or firmware, but a driver fault can look exactly the same. The timing is the best clue:

When it happensWhat it points to
On the motherboard logo or BIOS screenA hardware fault on the card, since no driver is running yet
Only under load, worse as the card warmsA memory chip or memory supply that fails with heat or high data rates
After a driver update, and a different card does it tooSoftware or another part of the computer

Before sending the card, strip the driver out using Display Driver Uninstaller (DDU) and reinstall it, undo any overclock, and try another computer if you can. Our GPU artefacting guide has the full list of checks.

Founders Edition owners: try PCIe 4.0 before sending the card. In launch week, overclocker der8auer reported boot failures, lock-ups and no signal from an RTX 5080 Founders Edition in a PCIe 5.0 slot, which stopped once the slot was set to PCIe 4.0 (Profesional Review, HotHardware). Igor's Lab reported an RTX 50 Founders Edition that would not work with risers or adapters unless the link was limited to PCIe 4.0 or below. NVIDIA had not commented at the time. If removing a riser and setting Gen 4 cures your card, it may not need a repair.

RTX 5080 12V-2x6 Power Connector: Care and Repair

The RTX 5080 takes its 360W through one 16-pin 12V-2x6 connector, and a heat-damaged or broken socket on the card can be replaced from $370. 12V-2x6 is the updated form of the 16-pin socket that the RTX 4080 Super used under the 12VHPWR name.

What has been reported about RTX 5080 connectors:

  • February 2025: Tom's Hardware reported a Reddit user's unconfirmed claim of a 16-pin cable melting at the power supply end in a system with an ASUS RTX 5080; the card's end was reported unaffected.
  • Another claim linking melted connectors to a new RTX 5080 proved a false alarm; the damage was traced to an RTX 4090 (Notebookcheck).
  • December 2025: Tom's Hardware reported an RTX 5080 Founders Edition whose connector latch broke on the first unplug.

Tom's Hardware noted that these incidents had not formed a clear pattern. Connector care is still worth the minute it takes:

  • Seat it fully. Push until the latch clicks and no gap shows.
  • Give the cable a straight run. Avoid a tight bend at the plug; the Founders Edition socket is angled and recessed to help.
  • Cable choice. Hardware Busters recommends a native 12+4 pin cable from the power supply over adapters. With the boxed adapter, run each 8-pin plug from its own cable.
  • Unplug gently. Press the latch fully and pull straight, without rocking.
  • Look for heat marks. Browning or a warped housing means stop using the card and the cable, and replace the cable too: an overheated plug can damage a new socket.

The repair: the damaged socket is removed, the board pads are cleaned and checked for lifted copper, a new connector is fitted, and the 12V input is measured for shorts. The card then runs at full load while the connector is watched with a thermal camera.

RTX 5080 Power Stage Faults and Short Circuits

A failed power stage on an RTX 5080 usually shows up as a card that is not detected or a PC that powers off the instant you press the button, and repairs start from $430. Power stages turn the 12V input into the low voltages the GPU and memory use.

  • What reviewers found on partner boards. Hardware Busters counted a 10-phase core supply on a Galax 1-Click OC, and Igor's Lab 11 core phases on an MSI Expert. Both used Monolithic Power Systems MP87993 power stages run by an MP29816 controller. Layouts differ between models, so parts are matched to your board.
  • How a stage fails. It often shorts. On the 12V input that trips the power supply at once; on the core rail the fans may run with no picture.

The shorted rail is found by measuring each rail to ground unpowered. A small current-limited supply is then fed in, and a thermal camera shows which part warms. Once it is replaced, the other parts on that rail are tested and every rail is raised current-limited before the card sees full load.

Suspect a short? Stop powering the card. Each attempt in a normal computer can turn one failed part into several.

RTX 5080 Crashes, Shutdowns and Overheating

An RTX 5080 that crashes, throttles or shuts the computer down under load points to cooling, the card's own power delivery, or a power supply that cannot keep up with a 360W card. Check the cheap causes first:

  • Power supply. NVIDIA lists 850W minimum system power for the RTX 5080. An older or smaller unit can switch the PC off under load, which looks like a card fault.
  • Cooler contact. Temperatures that climb fast after a repaste, a water block fit or a cooler swap usually mean poor contact or wrong pad thickness.
  • Tuning. Undo overclocks, undervolts and raised power limits, then test at stock.

A thermal service with pads matched to your board starts from $330. A card that comes through every bench test costs you only the $150 diagnostic, and the results tell you to look elsewhere in the system.

RTX 5080 Cards With Missing ROPs

Some early RTX 5080 cards shipped with 104 ROPs instead of the specified 112; this is a manufacturing defect inside the GPU chip, so it cannot be repaired and should go back for a warranty replacement. ROPs (render output units) handle the last stage of drawing each frame, so a card missing some still works but runs slower.

What NVIDIA said:

  • In February 2025 NVIDIA said under 0.5% of RTX 5090, 5090D and 5070 Ti GPUs had fewer ROPs than specified, with an average graphics performance impact of 4% and none on AI and compute work. Affected owners could contact the board manufacturer for a replacement, and the production anomaly had been corrected.
  • Days later NVIDIA told The Verge that an early production build of RTX 5080 GPUs was affected too.

How to check your card:

  1. Open GPU-Z on the Graphics Card tab and read the ROPs field.
  2. A correct RTX 5080 shows 112; an affected card shows 104.
  3. If yours shows 104, ask the retailer or the card's maker for a replacement.

Please check this before sending us a card: no board-level repair can restore missing ROPs. If a diagnostic turns up a low count, we tell you before quoting anything.

How We Diagnose an RTX 5080

Every RTX 5080 is tested from the least invasive check to the most, and nothing is repaired until you approve a fixed quote.

  1. The warranty question. If the card should go back to the retailer, we say so before it is opened.
  2. Inspection under magnification of the 12V-2x6 socket, both sides of the board, Founders Edition board joints and any earlier work.
  3. Unpowered measurements of the 12V input and each rail to ground.
  4. Current-limited start from a bench supply, then each rail checked in order.
  5. Firmware. When the symptom calls for it, the BIOS is dumped and compared with the right file for that exact card.
  6. Memory and ROPs. All eight GDDR7 chips are tested at full speed, and the ROP count is read so a factory defect is caught before you spend money.
  7. Load and heat. Faults that need warmth are reproduced, and the power connector is checked with a thermal camera at full load.
  8. The quote. The failed part, the fix, a fixed price, and a straight answer on whether the card deserves the money.

What Can and Cannot Be Repaired on an RTX 5080

Most board-level faults on an RTX 5080 can be repaired, but faults inside the GB203 chip itself, including missing ROPs, cannot.

Usually repairable:

  • Melted, cracked or unlatched 12V-2x6 sockets
  • A failed GDDR7 chip, when the correct replacement can be sourced
  • Failed power stages and shorted capacitors
  • Corrupted or mismatched firmware
  • Knocked-off parts, damaged display ports and PCIe contacts
  • Overheating from poor cooler contact or worn pads

Not repairable, or rarely worth it:

  • Missing ROPs. A factory defect in the GPU: a warranty replacement matter.
  • A failed or cracked GPU core. The core cannot be repaired, and a replacement GB203 is not something we can promise to find.
  • Burn damage through the board's inner layers. Rarely dependable on a 360W card.
  • A damaged joint between Founders Edition boards where the replacement part cannot be sourced.
  • Heavy corrosion or damage from an earlier repair attempt.

When a card cannot be repaired, the cost to you is the $150 diagnostic plus any return postage. Nothing more is charged, and we explain what failed.

Is an RTX 5080 Worth Repairing Out of Warranty?

Usually, yes: the RTX 5080 sits second in NVIDIA's RTX 50 desktop range, and a single-fault repair costs a small share of another card. Hardware Busters reported in July 2026 that RTX 5080 prices had risen during the year, mainly on memory costs.

Weigh it up against recently sold listings for a working RTX 5080, not asking prices. As an illustration: if working RTX 5080s are selling for around $1,500 near you, spending from $370 on a connector or from $430 on a power stage is an easy call, and a memory chip from $530 still leaves a wide margin.

Worth it when the fault is a connector, a power stage, a single memory chip, firmware or cooling, and the warranty has expired or a claim was declined. A card you know is also a safer bet than a used one with no history.

Think twice when:

  • the core itself is dead, or burn damage goes through the board (see what can be repaired);
  • a needed part, such as the correct memory chip or a Founders Edition board connector, cannot be sourced; or
  • the card is still under warranty. Go to the retailer first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an RTX 5080 be repaired?

Usually, yes. Damaged 12V-2x6 connectors, failed power stages, firmware, physical damage and a failed GDDR7 chip can all be repaired at board level, with memory work depending on getting the correct chip. A failed GPU core, deep burn damage or a factory defect such as missing ROPs cannot be fixed. A $150 bench diagnostic sorts out which group your card is in, and it is waived when you proceed.

My RTX 5080 is still under warranty. Should I send it to you?

Can you replace GDDR7 memory on an RTX 5080?

Can you fix an RTX 5080 with missing ROPs?

No. Some early RTX 5080s shipped with 104 ROPs instead of 112, and NVIDIA confirmed an early production build was affected. The missing units are inside the GPU chip, so no repair can restore them. Check the ROPs field in GPU-Z, and if it shows 104, ask the retailer or the card's maker for a replacement.
