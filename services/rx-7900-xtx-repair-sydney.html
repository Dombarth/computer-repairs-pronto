Navi 31 Chiplet Design: What It Means for Repairs

Both cards use AMD's Navi 31, built from chiplets: one graphics die and six memory cache dies on a single package, which for repair purposes is one part that either works or does not. AMD makes the two kinds of die on different processes:

PartWhat it doesProcess and size (AMD)
Graphics compute die (GCD)The graphics engine: up to 96 compute units5nm, 306mm²
Memory cache dies (MCDs)Six small dies, each with a GDDR6 memory interface and up to 16MB of Infinity Cache6nm, 37.5mm² each
PackageCarries the GCD and MCDs, joined by AMD's Infinity Links in fanout packagingBoth together, soldered to the board as one part
  • No single die can be swapped. If the GCD, an MCD or a link between them fails, the whole package has failed.
  • The package is not a practical repair part. New Navi 31 packages are not sold as spares, so the only source would be another card with an unknown history. In practice a failed package means a warranty claim or a replacement card, not a repair.
  • Memory faults need careful tracing. Each MCD drives a 64-bit slice of the 384-bit bus: two of the twelve memory chips. Errors on one slice could be a chip or its MCD, and testing must tell them apart before a chip is changed.
  • Reballing has limits. It renews the joints between package and board; it cannot fix a fault inside the package.

The RX 7900 XT has five MCDs active; Tom's Hardware reported the sixth position holds dummy silicon or a disabled die.

RX 7900 XTX Hotspot vs Edge Temperature

The GPU temperature (often labelled "edge") is a general reading for the chip, while the hotspot or junction temperature is the hottest point found by sensors spread across the die, so the hotspot always reads higher and the gap between them shows how well the cooler is working. Tools such as HWiNFO show both, usually with a memory temperature.

A high hotspot alone is not a fault. Writing about its RX 5700 series in 2019, AMD said running at up to 110°C junction temperature in typical gaming "is expected and within spec", because the GPU raises clocks until a sensor reaches its limit. Read the numbers together:

What the readings showWhat it usually means
Hotspot high but steady, moderate gap, clocks holdingA card working hard, as designed
Hotspot pinned at its limit within minutes, clocks falling, fans flat out, GPU temperature far lowerHeat is not leaving the die: paste, mounting, a pad holding the cooler off, or on a reference XTX the vapour chamber
Gap has widened over months or yearsDried or squeezed-out paste; a thermal service usually fixes it
Hotspot jumped after the cooler was last removedUneven mounting, poor paste coverage or a wrong-thickness pad
Memory temperature high tooPad contact over the GDDR6 chips

Logging GPU, hotspot and memory temperatures, clock and fan speed through fifteen minutes of a demanding game, and sending the numbers with your enquiry, usually shows which row applies.

The Reference RX 7900 XTX Vapour Chamber Issue

In late 2022 and early 2023, owners of some AMD reference RX 7900 XTX cards reported 110°C hotspot temperatures with heavy throttling, and AMD said the problem related to the reference card's thermal solution and affected a limited number of cards. What was reported, and by whom:

  • Reports: owners of reference cards, sold as "Made by AMD" (MBA) under AMD's and partners' names, saw 110°C hotspots and throttling, some with fans at full speed. TechSpot reported some early RMA requests were refused on the grounds that 110°C was normal.
  • Testing: overclocker Roman "der8auer" Hartung concluded the vapour chamber was the likely cause, and Igor's Lab reported several chamber production batches appeared to be involved. PC Gamer noted affected cards showed a much larger gap between average GPU temperature and hotspot.
  • AMD's statement (early January 2023, to Tom's Hardware and others): AMD believed the issue "relates to the thermal solution used in the AMD reference design and appears to be present in a limited number of the cards sold", and asked affected customers to contact AMD Support.
  • Cause: at CES 2023, AMD's Scott Herkelman told PCWorld a small batch of vapour chambers did not contain enough liquid. As reported by KitGuru and HotHardware, he called it a small percentage, said there was no safety concern, and told owners to contact AMD or the card's maker for a replacement.
  • Not affected: partner cards with their own coolers were not part of the reports, and Tom's Hardware reported AMD saying the reference RX 7900 XT was unaffected.

Why this is a cooler or warranty matter, not a board repair:

  • A vapour chamber is a sealed copper chamber with a little liquid inside that boils at the hot spot and condenses near the fins. With too little liquid inside it cannot move heat properly, and no circuit board work changes that.
  • Fresh paste and pads do not fix it, and we will not sell a $330 thermal service as a cure.
  • The fix is a working cooler: through the retailer while under warranty, or a replacement cooler for that model if one can be found.
  • The bench can confirm the cause: paste, mounting and pads are ruled out first, and you get the findings to take to the retailer.

A reference card that throttled from new matches the 2023 pattern; one that slowly got hotter over years points to worn paste.

RX 7900 XTX No Display Repair

An RX 7900 card with no picture usually has a supply rail that is not starting, corrupted or mismatched firmware, or an output-side fault, and each is repairable when the GPU package is healthy.

What the computer doesWhat it points to
Lists the card, but the screen stays blackPower is partly up: firmware, memory or output circuitry
Does not see the cardA rail not starting, or damage at the PCIe edge connector
Sees it on some starts onlyA heat-sensitive joint, dirty PCIe contacts or a marginal rail

Igor's Lab counted separate rails on the reference board for the graphics core, SoC, memory and memory interface. If one fails to start the card stays dark, yet the fans still turn, because they are fed from the 12V input rather than from those rails. First try another output and cable, reseat the card and push each 8-pin plug fully home.

Artefacts such as coloured blocks, lines, sparkles or corrupted textures most often point to a GDDR6 chip, but because Navi 31's memory controllers sit on the memory cache dies, testing must separate a chip fault from a package fault.

Errors spread across both chips of one slice can point to the memory cache die instead; if testing confirms that, the fault is inside the package and not repairable. RX 7900 XTX GDDR6 Memory Layout and Chip Replacement

The XTX carries twelve 2GB GDDR6 chips around the GPU package and the XT ten, and a failed chip can be replaced on its own from $530, subject to the correct chip being available.

  • Placement: TechPowerUp's look at the reference board shows the package surrounded by twelve GDDR6 chips, and Igor's Lab notes they are cooled directly by the base of the vapour chamber.
  • Chips: 24GB over twelve chips is 2GB each, rated at 20Gbps. Igor's Lab found SK hynix parts on its reference samples; makers can use other suppliers, so a replacement is matched to what is on your board.
  • The XT: ten chips on a 320-bit bus, matching its five working MCDs.
  1. Testing at temperature identifies the chip and confirms the errors follow it, not the whole slice.
  2. The board is preheated and the chip lifted with controlled hot air, shielding the parts around it.
  3. The pads are cleaned and prepared, and the new chip fitted.
  4. Every chip is retested warm, not only the new one.
  5. Memory pads are renewed at the measured thickness and the cooler refitted with fresh paste.

If the chip must be ordered, you are told the wait before approving the quote.

RX 7900 XTX Power Stage and VRM Repair

AMD rates the XTX at 355W of total board power and the XT at 315W, most of it passing through a multi-phase regulator whose power stages can fail short; repairs start from $430. Igor's Lab mapped the reference boards:

The reference controllers and power stages are Monolithic Power Systems parts; partner boards use their own designs, so parts are matched to the board. After the failed stage is swapped, the stages beside it, the controller and the rail capacitors are tested, the rail is first powered from a current-limited supply, and only then does the card run a long full-power load.

RX 7900 XTX Short Circuit Diagnosis

A short is located with the power off: each rail is measured to ground to find the shorted one, then a small current-limited supply is fed in while a thermal camera shows which part warms.

  • On the 12V input: the power supply trips the instant the PC is switched on; usually a power stage or input capacitor.
  • On the core, SoC or memory rail: fans may spin while the card stays dark or undetected.

If you suspect a short, stop powering the card. A shorted card switched on again and again can take out parts that were healthy.

RX 7900 XTX Driver Timeouts: Software or Hardware?

A driver timeout means Windows stopped waiting for the GPU and reset its driver; it can come from the driver, unstable tuning, the power supply or the card, and a few checks separate them. Microsoft calls this Timeout Detection and Recovery: if the GPU does not respond within two seconds by default, Windows resets it, the screen flickers or goes black, and AMD Software may report a driver timeout.

  1. Return any overclock, undervolt, power-limit or fan-curve change in AMD Software to default.
  2. Remove the driver with AMD's Cleanup Utility (AMD recommends Safe Mode), then install the current driver.
  3. Run each 8-pin input from its own power supply cable.
  4. Log hotspot and memory temperatures; if the hotspot is pinned just before each timeout, see hotspot vs edge temperature.
  5. Try the card in another computer. If the timeouts follow it, it needs bench diagnosis.

On the bench, memory is tested warm, each rail checked under load and the firmware compared with the correct version. A card that comes through all of that clean costs the $150 diagnostic, and the test log tells you to look elsewhere in the PC.

How We Diagnose an RX 7900 XTX or XT

Testing starts with checks that cannot harm the card and moves to powered tests only once it is safe. Not every step is needed for every symptom.

  1. Inspection of both sides under magnification: connectors, burn marks, liquid, pads, earlier repairs.
  2. Cold measurements of the 12V input and every rail to ground.
  3. Current-limited power-up, so a fault cannot draw enough to do more harm.
  4. Rails in sequence: core, SoC, memory and memory interface.
  5. Firmware read and compared with the correct version.
  6. Memory testing warm and cold, errors mapped to a chip or an MCD slice.
  7. Thermal check: temperatures logged under load, then the paste spread and pad imprints inspected for contact.
  8. Quote: what failed, the fixed price and whether we think the card justifies it. You approve before any work.

Can an RX 7900 XTX Be Repaired?

Yes, in most cases, because most faults are on the board around the GPU package rather than inside it. Usually repairable:

  • Failed GDDR6 memory chips
  • Failed power stages and other regulator parts
  • Shorted or cracked capacitors and small components
  • Scorched 8-pin connectors, damaged display ports and PCIe contacts
  • Corrupted or mismatched firmware
  • Worn paste and pads pushing up hotspot or memory temperature

What RX 7900 XTX Faults Cannot Be Repaired?

A fault inside the Navi 31 package, an underfilled reference vapour chamber and severe board damage are where a bench repair is impossible or not worth paying for.

  • A failed graphics or memory cache die: warranty or replacement.
  • A faulty vapour chamber: a working cooler is the answer, not board work.
  • Burning through the board's layers, rarely reliable to repair on a card drawing over 300W.
  • Heavy corrosion, a cracked board, or several major faults together.
  • A previous repair gone wrong, for example lifted solder pads beneath a memory chip or the package.

You are told after the diagnostic and pay the $150 and any return postage, nothing else, with an explanation of what failed.

Is an RX 7900 XTX Worth Repairing?

Usually, when the fault is a single repair: a thermal service, power stage, connector or one memory chip normally costs a fraction of what a working 24GB card sells for. Check what working XTX and XT cards have actually sold for recently; asking prices run high.

  • Worth it: one fault, a healthy package, and a quote well below a used card with an unknown history. Our replacement parts come with a 12-month warranty for the same fault.
  • Maybe not: a failed package, a badly burnt board or several faults adding up. The XT sells for less than the XTX, so its margin runs out sooner; we will say so.
  • Not a repair job: a reference XTX with a faulty vapour chamber, or any card still under warranty. Start with the retailer, because your rights under the Australian Consumer Law consumer guarantees are with them.

As an illustration: if working RX 7900 XTX cards are selling for around $1,000 near you, a memory chip from $530 or a power stage from $430 costs about half that or less, on a card whose history you already know.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an RX 7900 XTX be repaired?

Yes, in most cases. Failed GDDR6 chips, power stages, scorched 8-pin connectors, damaged ports and firmware can all be repaired at component level. A fault inside the Navi 31 package or a faulty reference vapour chamber cannot be fixed by board repair. Diagnosis settles which, for $150 that is waived when you approve the repair.

Why does my RX 7900 XTX hotspot reach 110°C?

The hotspot is the hottest point on the die, so it always reads above the GPU temperature, and AMD has said (of its earlier RX 5700 series) that up to 110°C junction in gaming is within spec. It becomes a problem when it sits at the limit with clocks falling and a large gap to the GPU temperature: think paste, mounting or, on some reference XTX cards, the vapour chamber.

Is my reference RX 7900 XTX affected by the vapour chamber problem?

AMD said in early 2023 that the issue related to the reference design's thermal solution and affected a limited number of XTX cards. Partner coolers were not part of the reports. A reference card that has throttled at a 110°C hotspot since new fits the pattern; one that got hotter slowly over years more likely needs fresh paste.

Will new thermal paste fix a faulty vapour chamber?

No. AMD's Scott Herkelman said affected chambers did not contain enough liquid, which is sealed inside the cooler. The fix is a working cooler, through the retailer while the card is under warranty. We can confirm the cause, but we will not sell a thermal service as a cure.

Can the GPU chip on an RX 7900 XTX be replaced?

Not as a practical repair. Navi 31 is one graphics die and six memory cache dies bonded into a single package, so no die can be swapped, and new packages are not sold as parts. A failed package is a warranty or replacement matter.
