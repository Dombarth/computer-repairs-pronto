Why the same repair costs more on some laptops

The part and how deep it sits inside the laptop set the price, and both change from model to model.

  • Keyboards fixed to the palm rest. On many thin laptops the keyboard is not a separate part. On the Dell Inspiron 14 5430, for example, Dell's service manual replaces the whole palm-rest and keyboard assembly, and the system board and display come out first.
  • One model, several screens. Lenovo lists five different panels for the ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12, from a 60Hz 1920 x 1200 screen to a 120Hz touch OLED. We order by the part number on the panel, not the model name.
  • Screens that only come as a unit. On some 2-in-1s the display cannot be taken apart. Dell says any fault in the Latitude 9450 2-in-1 display means replacing the whole assembly.
  • Memory on the board. Thin laptops often have soldered memory. On the X1 Carbon Gen 12 it is soldered and not upgradable, so a memory fault is a board-level job and more memory is not an option.

Is it worth repairing your laptop?

Usually yes when the repair is a small part of the cost of a similar new laptop and the laptop still does what you need. Two more things count:

  • Windows support. Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025. Windows 11 needs TPM 2.0, Secure Boot capable UEFI, 4GB of memory and a supported 64-bit processor. A laptop that cannot run Windows 11 is a weaker candidate for a big repair.
  • Your time. A repair keeps your files, programs and settings. A new laptop means moving all of that across.

A laptop that won't turn on is often one failed part

Most no-power and no-charging faults sit in the power path: the charger, the port, or a part on the motherboard such as a MOSFET (a small power switch) or the charging chip. We find the failed part and replace it, rather than replacing the whole board. Board repairs start at $329.

Before you bring your laptop in

Five minutes now can save your files and a second trip.

  1. Find your BitLocker recovery key. If you sign in to Windows with a Microsoft account, device encryption may already be on. A motherboard repair or a BIOS update can make Windows ask for the 48-digit key, and Microsoft cannot recreate a lost one. Look it up at aka.ms/myrecoverykey and write it down.
  2. Note the model. It is on the label underneath (on Dell laptops, the 7-character Service Tag). We need it to order the right part.
  3. Back up if it still runs. Most repairs do not touch your files, but a copy costs nothing.
  4. Bring the charger. We test with it, because the charger is often part of the problem.
  5. Check your warranty first. If the laptop is still under the maker's warranty, or recent enough for a claim under the Australian Consumer Law, contact the seller first. The ACCC says consumer guarantees can apply after a warranty has ended.
