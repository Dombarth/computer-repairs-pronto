Tamworth heat, storms and frosts

The Bureau of Meteorology's Tamworth Airport station (055325) records a mean January maximum of 33.1 °C, with December at 31.0 °C and February at 31.7 °C. It logs 27.1 days a year that reach 35 °C, and on 12 February 2017 it reached 45.9 °C, its highest on record. A PC in a room like that breathes hot air all afternoon, and over a few summers the paste dries and the pads harden until the card throttles or crashes under load. The cure is a thermal service, from $330. Dust matted into the cooler fins makes it worse.

Storms bring a different failure. The severe thunderstorm that hit just before 4 pm on 26 November 2025 brought winds above 125 km/h, and wind, lightning and falling trees brought down lines; outages peaked at about 8,000 customers that evening, worst in North Tamworth, Westdale and Taminda. A mains surge can destroy the power stages supplying a card's core and memory, leaving a card that will not power up properly; that repair starts from $430. Storm season, as NSW SES defines it, is September to April, and its 2023 report on storm risk says about 90% of the state's yearly severe thunderstorm activity lands between October and March. Unplugging the computer before severe weather arrives is the advice from both SES and Essential Energy.

Winter swings the other way. The same station averages 27.6 days a year when it drops to 0 °C or below, and July's mean minimum is 2.4 °C. If a card has spent the night in a cold car or shed, let it reach room temperature before you fit it and switch on, because warm room air meeting a cold board can leave condensation.

Essential Energy runs the network here and pays for damage only where its own negligence caused it. Storms are outside its control, so a surge claim normally starts with your home and contents insurer, and both want paperwork: its claim form wants copies of repair invoices, quotes or an electrician's report, and our emailed diagnosis and quote records what failed and what it costs to put right. If a network turns down a claim, EWON, the energy and water ombudsman, can be asked to review it. We cannot promise how a claim will end.

Posting a card from Tamworth

  • Straight to the counter in summer. Do not leave the parcel in a parked car on a 35 °C afternoon.
  • Keep the lodgement receipt. Guarantee or not, the counter gives you the proof of lodgement a claim needs. Keep proof of the card's value too, and lodge any claim inside six months.
  • Pack for up to four days on the road. Anti-static bag, a snug rigid inner box, then an outer box with 7cm or more of padding on every side. Shake it and listen. Australia Post pays nothing on a card it finds was not adequately packed.
  • Mind the courier's limits. Sendle's extra cover stops at $1,500, well short of a card you value at $3,600, and CouriersPlease leaves electronic devices out of its included cover.

Send the card by itself with its cooler on, in a plain box; leave the leads, brackets and retail packaging at home. For the return leg, either enclose a prepaid label or let us add the postage, at cost, to your invoice. The full packing steps are on our mail-in repair hub.

A card still under warranty

If the card is recent and you bought it new from a business, go back to that retailer before posting it anywhere. Under the Australian Consumer Law, a major fault means you choose whether to take a refund or a replacement, and the shop must handle it itself instead of pointing you to the manufacturer. The maker's warranty is extra, never a substitute. None of this covers misuse or a card bought privately, which is where a paid repair makes sense.

Questions

Does anyone in Tamworth repair graphics cards at board level?

Not that we could find. In our September 2026 search, no Tamworth business advertised graphics card or board-level repair, and none published a GPU price or warranty. The local computer shops handle builds, upgrades and general repairs, and they are a sensible first stop for driver trouble or a card still under warranty. For a failed power stage, memory chip or connector, posting the card to our workshop near Castle Hill is the practical option, with repairs from $330.

How long will my card be away from Tamworth?

For a thermal service sent Express both ways, allow three to seven business days between posting and getting it back, on Australia Post's estimates, plus the time it takes you to say yes to the quote. Parcel Post in both directions stretches that to about seven to ten. A power stage or memory repair adds a few more days on the bench, and a reball more again. None of the postal figures is guaranteed, because Tamworth sits outside the Express network.

Do Tamworth summers wear out graphics cards?

Heat does not kill a card overnight, but it ages the paste and pads that move heat off the GPU and memory into the heatsink. Tamworth Airport averages 27.1 days a year of 35 °C-plus heat, so a PC in a room without air conditioning works hard for weeks at a time. Climbing temperatures, loud fans and crashes late in a long game are the usual signs. A thermal service starts from $330.

Could a thunderstorm surge have killed my graphics card?

It can happen. A surge on the mains can take out a card's power stages, and the 26 November 2025 storm brought power lines down, with North Tamworth, Westdale and Taminda among the worst hit. The diagnostic confirms whether that is what failed, and the emailed report is the kind of paperwork an insurer asks for. Power stage repair starts from $430. When the next storm is forecast, unplug the PC, as NSW SES and Essential Energy advise.

Is it better to repair my card or buy a new one?

That depends mostly on what the card is worth. For a recent RTX or Radeon card, repairs start from $330 and even a reball starts from $630, which often compares well with replacing it, and anything we replace stays under a 12-month warranty for the same fault. For a card that would sell for a couple of hundred dollars, postage from Tamworth plus the $150 diagnostic usually tips the balance the other way. Ring with the model and the symptoms and we will say which side of the line it falls.
