GPU & Graphics Card Repair Canberra: Posted or Driven to Our Sydney Bench

Yes. A graphics card (GPU) that has died, started artefacting or begun overheating in a Canberra PC can be posted to our workshop and repaired at component level. That means tracing the fault to the part that failed, whether a power stage, a memory chip, a connector or the thermal material, and replacing it. The card stays; only the broken bit goes.

To be clear about where the work happens: we are in Sydney, not Canberra. The bench is near Castle Hill in the Hills District, and board-level repair has been our trade there since 2006. Of all the capitals, Canberra is the closest to it. Repairs start from $330. The $150 bench diagnostic is waived in full when you go ahead, and whichever way the card reaches us, any parts we replace are warranted for a full 12 months against the original fault recurring. What a Canberra Search Turns Up

Search for graphics card repair in Canberra and the results are general PC shops and a national chain. When we looked in September 2026, no Canberra business doing board-level graphics card work ranked, and a Reddit comment that does rank says nobody in Canberra will reball a card. That does not prove no one local can, so put these questions to anyone before leaving a card, us included:

  • Will you find and replace the failed part, or is the answer a new card?
  • What does the diagnosis cost, and does it come off the repair?
  • How long is the warranty, and does it cover labour as well as parts?
  • Do you do reflow, reball and memory chip work?
  • What do I pay if the card cannot be saved?

Our answers, in order: component-level repair; $150, waived if you go ahead; 12 months, on the replaced parts only and for the same fault; yes, with reball from $630 quoted per card; the $150 plus return postage.

A local shop is the better first stop when the problem may not be the card at all: a driver fault, a loose power lead, a bad cable or monitor, or a PC that needs a clean or a replacement card fitted. Our guide to GPU artefacting runs through the checks that separate a card fault from everything else. Staying local also suits an inexpensive card: at a couple of hundred dollars second-hand, the diagnostic plus postage both ways is usually not worth spending, and we will say so on the phone.

Canberra Summers and the Faults They Bring

Canberra is better known for its winters, but the Bureau of Meteorology's mean maximums put January at 30.0°C, with December at 27.4°C and February at 28.0°C. That January figure is above Melbourne's and Adelaide's and less than half a degree short of Brisbane's.

A card in a room that warm draws in warmer air and leans harder on the paste and pads that carry heat from the chips to the cooler. As those dry out with age, the card runs hotter, throttles, or crashes in games while behaving itself on the desktop. The fix is a thermal service, from $330 and usually one to two business days on the bench. A hot summer alone is not a diagnosis, though, which is why the bench diagnostic comes first.

Plenty of faults owe nothing to the weather: a melted 12VHPWR or 12V-2x6 connector is connector work from $370, and a card dead after a BIOS update is firmware recovery from $330.

Packing and Posting From Canberra

  • Weigh the one-day gap. Parcel Post is estimated at two business days from Canberra and Express at one, so the slower service costs roughly a day each way. If this is your only card, Express earns its place. If a spare is keeping the PC running, Parcel Post gives up little.
  • Pack for a long trip anyway. Australia Post recommends a snug, rigid box packed box-in-box with cushioning; Sendle suggests an anti-static bag, at least 7cm of cushioning and a shake test. A "Fragile" label is not a safeguard.
  • Keep the outside plain. Aramex advises against a box that advertises electronics. Inside goes the card only, cooler fitted, with the job number; cables, brackets and the retail box stay in Canberra.
  • Lodge at the counter and keep the receipt. A claim needs proof of lodgement and proof of value, and must be made within six months. The sender claims for loss; the recipient claims for damage by showing the card and packaging at a Post Office, so if the return parcel reaches you crushed, keep every piece. Australia Post pays nothing if the packing was inadequate.
  • Check a courier's cover first. Sendle includes $100 and sells extra cover up to $1,500, short of a dearer card. CouriersPlease's included cover excludes electronic devices, so it does not protect a graphics card.

The complete packing checklist, including water-cooled cards and what to write in the note, is on the mail-in repair hub.

A Recent Card Goes Back to the Retailer First

Under the Australian Consumer Law, a manufacturer's warranty sits on top of the consumer guarantees rather than replacing them, and the guarantees can outlast it. For a major failure you choose a refund or a replacement, the claim goes to the retailer, and a business must not send you off to the manufacturer. Misuse and private sales fall outside the guarantees. In the ACCC's 2018 Federal Court case against Apple ($9 million in penalties), the court found that repair by someone else cannot by itself end the consumer guarantees. A manufacturer's own warranty is a separate matter, so claim before anyone opens the card.

Questions

Can I drop my card off instead of posting it?

Yes, by arrangement. Canberra is about three hours from our workshop near Castle Hill by road, so if you are making the trip to Sydney anyway, the card can travel with you. Ring 02 8428 9224 or fill in the form first. The address is not published; we email it with a job number and agree a time. Where can I get a graphics card repaired in Canberra?

For driver problems, installation or swapping in a new card, a general Canberra PC shop will do. For board-level work, such as replacing a power stage or memory chip or reballing, we found no Canberra specialist in the search results, so the practical option is to post the card to a workshop that does it. Ours is in Sydney: Express Post is estimated at one business day each way, and repairs start from $330.

Do you reball graphics cards?

Yes. Reflow and reball work starts from $630 and is quoted per card once the diagnostic is done. It usually takes five to ten business days on the bench. Because the $150 diagnostic comes first, you know what has failed and what the fixed price is before any solder is touched, and if a reball is the wrong fix for your card, we tell you rather than do it anyway.

How long does it take to post a GPU from Canberra?

Australia Post's delivery estimator puts Canberra to Sydney Metro at one business day by Express Post and two by Parcel Post, and the return leg is similar. Those are estimates. Express Post also has a next-business-day guarantee between capital cities, provided it is posted Monday to Friday before the Post Office closes, and not in a red street box or Parcel Locker. Add three to seven business days on the bench for a power stage or memory repair.

Can GPUs be repaired or should I replace it?

Often a card stops working because one part on the board has failed, and that part can be replaced. Repair makes sense when the card is worth well over the cost of the job, and cards from the RTX 30, 40 and 50 series or Radeon RX 6000 to 9000 series most often clear that bar. A card that would fetch a couple of hundred dollars second-hand usually is not, once the $150 diagnostic and postage are added, and we will tell you before you send it.
