Coastal weather in Ballina and how cards fail in it

Ballina Airport AWS (station 058198, 1992–2026) logs 1,782.0 mm of rain a year and January humidity of 74% at 9am, 67% at 3pm. January's mean maximum is a moderate 28.4 °C, with 1.7 days a year reaching 35 °C, though the station hit 42.0 °C on 12 January 2002. Salty, humid air off the surf is a general hazard for electronics. Heat and dust are better understood: dried paste and pads, clogged fins, loud fans and falling clocks, usually put right by a thermal service, from $330. July nights average 8.8 °C; leave a card that arrives home cold unpowered until it has reached room temperature, as cold parts in warm, damp air can gather condensation.

Ballina Shire Council records major Richmond River floods affecting Ballina and Wardell in 1954, 1974 and 2022; in March 2022, local creeks and overland flooding also reached parts of Ballina and the Teven Valley. At Woodburn the river peaked 1.8 m above its 1954 record. If your PC took on water, leave it unplugged: NSW SES and Essential Energy want flood-affected gear passed by a licensed electrician first, and no workshop, ours included, stands in for that. Floodwater often leaves a card beyond economic repair; the $150 diagnostic settles it.

Power is the other coastal story. With ex-Tropical Cyclone Alfred passing in March 2025, Essential Energy reported just over 42,600 homes and businesses off supply at 11am on 8 March between Tweed Heads and Grafton, and 84,000 in all. Storms, says NSW SES, are a September-to-April affair across the state, with roughly 90% of the year's severe thunderstorm activity packed into October–March according to its 2023 hazard report. Both NSW SES and Essential Energy say to unplug the computer before severe weather; a surge can take a card's power stages with it, and those are replaced from $430.

Essential Energy says storm damage is not generally reimbursed and pays only where it was negligent, so ring your home and contents insurer first. Both want paperwork, such as an electrician's report or a repair quote, and the diagnosis and quote we send by email put the failed part and its repair price in writing. Outcomes are theirs; EWON can review a refused network claim.

Sending a card from Ballina or Byron Bay

  • Pack indoors, dry. On a sticky day the card stays in its sealed anti-static bag until it goes in the box.
  • Double-box. Snug rigid inner box, cushioning, outer box (Australia Post); padding of 7 cm or more plus a shake test (Sendle); plain cardboard that does not shout electronics (Aramex). "Fragile" is not a safeguard.
  • The card alone. Cooler fitted, job number enclosed; leads, brackets and retail packaging stay behind.
  • Post office before the surf. A sealed car at the beach in summer gets hot enough to bake a parcel.
  • Storm season. From October to March, bag the box against rain on the way in, and keep the counter receipt as proof of lodgement.

Returns can go to a PO Box, handy around Teven, Tintenbar and Newrybar. Tuck a prepaid return label inside, or return postage goes on the invoice at cost. For the full packing guide, see the mail-in hub.

Warranty, retailer and the law

If the card is recent, the seller hears about it before we do. The guarantees in the Australian Consumer Law work alongside the maker's warranty and cannot be taken away by it; the retailer, not the manufacturer, must deal with you, and for a major failure you choose refund or replacement. Misuse and private purchases are excluded. In 2018 the Federal Court, in ACCC v Apple, held that an independent repair does not on its own end your guarantees; what the manufacturer's warranty allows is a separate question.

Questions

Can I post a graphics card to you from Byron Bay?

Yes. Byron Bay (2481) sits in NSW Rural for Express, like Ballina, so Australia Post estimates two to three business days each way by Express and three to four by Parcel Post, with no next-day guarantee on that route. Pack it as described above, put the job number in the box, and say where it should come home: your street address, or a PO Box if that suits better.

Does Ballina's sea air damage gaming PCs?

Humid, salty coastal air is a general risk to electronics, but there is no measurement we can point to for how much it shortens a graphics card's life, so we will not guess. The causes we can name are heat and dust, which dry out thermal paste and pads and clog cooler fins in Ballina as anywhere else. If the fans are loud and clocks are dropping, a thermal service (from $330) usually sorts it.

Is swapping a graphics card the same as repairing it?

No. A swap means fitting a different card; the one that failed is written off. The only price we saw in Ballina's search results was a marketplace task offering $150 to assess and swap a card. A repair means finding the failed component, such as a power stage or memory chip, and replacing just that, so your own card keeps working. Ours start from $330 after a $150 diagnostic, which comes off the bill when you say yes.

What does graphics card repair cost from Ballina, postage and cover included?

Repairs are from $330 (thermal or firmware), from $370 (connectors), from $430 (power stages), from $530 (memory) and from $630 (reball). You pay the $150 diagnostic only when no repair follows. Postage is yours each way at the service you pick. For a card you value at $3,800, Extra Cover is $92.50 per leg, and Signature on Delivery adds $2.95 because the value is over $500.

My PC died during the cyclone power cuts. Is the graphics card finished?

Not necessarily. NSW SES notes that a storm can cause power surges, and surges are one way a card's power stages die; we replace those from $430. The damage may also sit in the power supply or motherboard, so test the card in a second machine if one is available. Next time the power goes, switch off and unplug, as NSW SES advises. For an insurance claim, the diagnosis we email is written evidence of the failed part and the repair price.
