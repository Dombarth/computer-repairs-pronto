Why a Faulty Card Usually Is Not Dead

A modern GPU is a large circuit board carrying several hundred components. The processor itself sits in the middle, surrounded by memory chips, and along one edge sits a bank of power delivery circuitry that converts what comes in from your PSU into the low voltages the processor actually runs on.

Almost all of those parts fail more readily than the processor does. A power stage MOSFET is a cheap component under constant thermal stress, memory chips sit under a heatsink that may not have been making proper contact for years, and solder joints crack with repeated heating and cooling. When one gives out the card stops working, but the expensive part in the middle is usually untouched. Replace the failed component and the card goes back to normal.

Working Out Whether It Is Worth Fixing

This is the question worth answering before anything else, and the honest answer depends almost entirely on what the card is worth. Cooling work pays for itself on nearly any card. Power and memory faults make sense on cards in the mid range and above. A degrading processor is not worth touching regardless of what the card cost.

Replacement pricing has made this calculation far more favourable than it was a few years ago. Supply has been tight through 2025 and 2026 and Australian prices climbed accordingly, with the RTX 5090 averaging above $5,000. Telling a Failing Card from Other Problems

Plenty of things look like a dying card and are not. Before assuming the worst, reseat the card, try a different cable and monitor, and if the machine has onboard graphics, run from that instead. Driver problems produce black screens and crashes that mimic hardware failure closely.

The signs pointing at genuine hardware failure are corruption that persists into the BIOS screen before drivers ever load, corruption that changes with temperature, a card drawing heavily but producing no output, and burning or hot spots you can smell. Corruption in one game only is usually software. Corruption at the BIOS screen is not. Our guide to GPU artifacting walks through the whole diagnostic sequence if you want to rule things out yourself first.

How Long Cards Last

Most cards run five to eight years of normal use before anything fails, and plenty last well beyond that. Cards are usually replaced because they can no longer run current software well, not because they have broken.

Heat and dust shorten it more than anything else. Ex-mining cards are an interesting case: they tend to be in better condition than people expect, because steady load at moderate temperatures is gentler than repeated heating and cooling. When they do fail it is usually the power section or the pads, not the processor.

Questions

Can a graphics card be repaired?

In most cases yes. Cards normally fail because one component on the board has given out, not because the whole thing is finished. Replace that part and the card works again. The exception is the processor degrading internally, which is not economically repairable on any card.

Is it worth repairing a graphics card?

It depends on the fault and what the card is worth. Cooling work is worth doing on almost anything. Power and memory faults make sense on mid range cards and above. Processor faults rarely make sense on anything. We give you that assessment before you commit, including when the answer is no.

How much is it to repair a graphics card?

Thermal work starts from $430, power stage repairs from $530, memory chip replacement from $630 and reball work from $730. The diagnostic is $150 and we waive it in full if you go ahead with the repair, so you are only ever out of pocket on a card you choose not to fix.

How to tell if a GPU is burnt out?

Look for discoloured or cracked components near the power section, a burnt smell, scorch marks around the power connectors, or a card drawing heavy current the moment it powers on. Corruption that appears at the BIOS screen before drivers load also points at hardware rather than software.

What is the lifespan of a GPU?

Five to eight years of normal use is typical and many go longer. Heat and dust shorten it more than anything else. Most cards are retired because they can no longer run current software well rather than because they have failed.

Is there a GPU shortage in Australia?

Supply has been tight and prices have risen through 2025 and 2026, with reported production cuts on the RTX 50 series pushing Australian averages up sharply. That is part of why repair is worth considering. When replacements cost this much, fixing a card with one failed part makes more sense than it did two years ago.

How do you fix a card that has failed?

It depends entirely on which part failed. Power section faults mean replacing MOSFETs or rebuilding a phase. Memory faults mean replacing chips and retesting. Cracked joints mean reflow or reball work. Firmware faults mean reprogramming the flash chip. The diagnosis determines the method, which is why the assessment comes first.

Is a reflow just a temporary fix?

Sometimes, and we will say so before you pay. A cracked joint that reflows properly can last years. A processor degrading internally will fail again no matter what we do, and in that case we tell you to replace the card rather than take your money.

What warranty do you give on the repair?

12 months on parts and labour, on every graphics card repair. Most board level shops offer 90 days. If the fault we repaired returns inside that year, bring it back and we sort it out at no charge.

How often do repairs actually work?

Around nine in ten cards we take on go home working. The main reason it is that high is that we assess first and decline the ones we do not think will hold — a degrading graphics processor gets sent home rather than reballed. If yours is in the ten percent, you pay the $150 diagnostic and nothing more.

Will this void my warranty?

If the card is still under manufacturer warranty, use that first — it will be cheaper. This is for cards out of warranty, bought second hand, already opened, or where the RMA was knocked back.

Do you work on laptop graphics chips?

Yes. In a laptop the graphics processor is soldered to the mainboard, so it is board level work rather than a card swap. See board level repair for that side of things.

What does the diagnostic cost?

$150, and it is waived in full if you go ahead with the repair. It covers proper bench testing rather than a guess from the symptom, which is what tells you whether the card is worth fixing at all. Rough guidance over the phone costs nothing.
