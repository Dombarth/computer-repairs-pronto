Riverina heat, dust and storms, and the faults they leave

At the Bureau of Meteorology's Wagga Wagga AMO station, the mean January maximum is 31.9 °C, 20.6 days a year reach 35 °C or more, and the record is 46.1 °C, from 4 January 2020. A gaming PC in a room that bakes all summer keeps its card hot for months, and heat dries out thermal paste and pads. The symptoms are rising temperatures, fans roaring at light load and clocks that sag in long sessions. A thermal service, from $330, fixes it.

Dust does the same job by another route: it clogs the cooler, and the card overheats. After a day like the dust storm of 21 September 2019, when warnings went out about visibility and breathing, check the case filters and the card's fins.

Storms are the sharper risk. On 15 January 2025, a severe thunderstorm put a 106 km/h gust through Wagga Airport and left more than 16,000 Essential Energy customers without power, from Gobbagombalin and Bomen to Lake Albert and Forest Hill. Surges can destroy a card's power stages, a fault we repair from $430. NSW SES reckons storm season runs September to April, with about 90% of severe thunderstorm activity from October to March, and the SES says to pull the plug on computers and consoles before the weather turns, with Essential Energy naming computers too.

Worth knowing: Essential Energy compensates only when a loss stems from its own negligence, and storm damage is generally not reimbursed. Your home and contents policy is usually the place to start. The diagnosis and quote we email you name the failed parts and the repair cost, which is the kind of evidence insurers look for; Essential Energy, for its part, wants repair invoices, quotations or an electrician's report. No claim is guaranteed, and EWON can review one that is refused.

The Murrumbidgee reached 10.56 m in March 2012 and went over the North Wagga levee; in November 2022 it peaked at 9.72 m, and more than 700 residents were evacuated from North and East Wagga. If a computer has been in floodwater, do not switch it on. It needs a licensed electrician's check before use, which our workshop cannot stand in for, and cards that have soaked in floodwater are frequently not worth saving. The diagnostic settles it.

Sending a card from 2650

  • Drive it straight to the counter. On a 35 °C day, do not leave the boxed card in a parked car while you shop. Lodge it over the Post Office counter and keep the receipt.
  • Double-box it. Anti-static bag, a snug rigid inner carton, and an outer carton with no less than 7cm of cushioning around it. Shake the parcel; nothing should shift. "Fragile" written on the outside protects nothing.
  • Keep it plain. The card only, cooler fitted, in a box that does not advertise electronics. Leave cables, brackets and the retail box in Wagga.
  • Job number inside, return address agreed. Post once our email arrives, and tell us whether the card should return to a PO Box or to where you live.

The complete packing steps are on the mail-in hub.

Recent purchases: start with the seller

A recently bought card should go back to the retailer before it comes to us. Under Australian Consumer Law, a manufacturer warranty sits on top of your consumer guarantees and cannot shrink them. With a major failure, the choice between refund and replacement is yours, and the seller has to honour it rather than send you to the manufacturer. Cards bought privately, or misused, are not covered. In 2018 the Federal Court, deciding ACCC v Apple, found that an outside repair does not, by that alone, end the guarantees, but the retailer is still the first call.
