Tableland cold, summer storms and the card inside your PC

At 642 m, Goulburn is cold country. The Bureau of Meteorology's Goulburn Airport AWS averages 94.9 nights a year dropping to 2 °C or lower and 60.6 reaching freezing, with a July mean minimum of 0.4 °C and a record low of −10.9 °C on 17 August 1994. A card or PC carried from a car, shed or cold spare room into a warm house can collect condensation; give it time to warm to room temperature before you switch on, a repaired card arriving in winter included.

Summers are warmer than the altitude suggests: a January mean maximum of 28.0 °C, 31.3 days a year reaching 30 °C, and a record 42.8 °C on 4 January 2020. Hot rooms and long sessions dry out paste and pads, and dust builds up in the fins; both end in a card that runs hot and throttles, the job for a thermal service from $330.

Storms bring the power risk. Severe storms with lightning on 27 January 2019 left more than 1,300 Essential Energy customers without supply across Goulburn, Nerriga, Braidwood and Tarago, and on 10 February 2025 storms cut power to 1,323 premises north and east of town, Taralga and Bungonia among them. Not every outage is weather: on 24 June 2025 fallen wires near Marys Mount blacked out 1,575 households. NSW SES's 2023 hazard report estimates that October to March accounts for about 90% of NSW severe thunderstorm activity. Its advice is to unplug sensitive appliances "such as computers, gaming consoles" before severe weather, and to unplug equipment once the power has gone. A surge can take out a card's power stages, repair work from $430.

Essential Energy runs Goulburn's network and compensates only where its own negligence caused the loss. Its claim form asks you to attach repair invoices, quotations, an electrician's report or similar, plus photos of the damaged equipment and its serial plate before disposal, so photograph the card before you post it. Home and contents insurance is usually the first claim to make. Our emailed diagnosis names the failed component and the quote prices the fix, the sort of record insurers and networks ask for, but no claim is guaranteed. EWON, the energy ombudsman, can look at a refused claim.

Sending it from Goulburn

  1. Double-box it. Snug and rigid on the inside, a cushioned outer box around it: that is Australia Post's advice. Sendle wants an anti-static bag, 7cm-plus of cushioning, and a box that passes a shake. Do not rely on a "Fragile" label.
  2. Plain outside, card only inside. Aramex's advice is a plain box. Cables, bracket and retail box stay in Goulburn; the cooler stays on the card, with the job number beside it.
  3. Lodge at the counter. The counter gives you proof of lodgement, which a claim needs along with proof of value within six months. Poorly packed items get nothing.
  4. Choose where it comes back to. Put a paid return label in with the card, or have the return postage invoiced at cost. Street addresses and PO Boxes both work.

A tip for Goulburn: if a courier is cheaper, check its cover. Sendle's included cover is $100, with extra available to $1,500, and CouriersPlease's included cover excludes electronic devices. The full packing guide is on our mail-in hub.

Consumer law in brief

If a recent card fails, the retailer or the manufacturer's warranty should be your first call, and that warranty adds to your Australian Consumer Law guarantees without removing any. A major failure entitles you to pick refund or replacement from the business that sold it, which cannot redirect you to the manufacturer. The guarantees exclude misuse and private sales. The Federal Court's ACCC v Apple decision of 2018, with $9 million in penalties, found that using an independent repairer does not, of itself, bring them to an end; the maker's warranty is a separate question.

Is a cheaper card worth sending from Goulburn?

Sometimes not, and we would rather tell you before you post it. If the card would sell second-hand for a couple of hundred dollars, two-way postage from Goulburn plus our $150 diagnostic usually outweighs it. Mid-range and higher cards, such as recent GeForce RTX or Radeon RX models, are usually worth it. Describe the card and the fault on 02 8428 9224, and the rough advice costs nothing.

Can a card that shows no signs of life be revived?

In many cases, yes. A card that is completely dead, or one whose fans roar up and then quit, can come down to a failed power stage, repair work from $430, or firmware that did not survive an update, from $330. The diagnostic tells us which. If the card cannot be saved you pay $150 plus return postage and get an explanation of the failure. Rule out the power supply and cables first.

Can you post the repaired card to a PO Box around Goulburn?

Yes. A PO Box suits people on rural properties around Taralga, Tarago or Crookwell, or anyone who is out when parcels come.
