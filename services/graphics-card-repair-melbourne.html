Melbourne Graphics Card Repair by Post

Yes. A graphics card from Melbourne can be repaired at component level by posting it to our bench in Sydney. We track down the failed part on the board, be it a MOSFET in the power stage, a GDDR memory chip, a melted power connector or a corrupted BIOS, and replace that part rather than write off the card. To avoid any confusion later: the bench is in Sydney, out in the Hills District by Castle Hill. There is no Melbourne shopfront and no Melbourne drop-off point.

Repairs start from $330. Every job begins with a $150 bench diagnostic, and that $150 is taken off the invoice in full if you approve the repair. Once finished, it is covered by our 12-month warranty on both parts and labour, no different from a card handed over in person. The mail-in repair page for all of Australia covers the general process; this page handles the Melbourne end.

On Australia Post's own estimate, an Express parcel from Melbourne reaches Sydney in one business day, so for most faults the bench time, not the post, decides when the card is back in your machine.

The Express Post guarantee from Melbourne

Melbourne and Sydney are both capitals on the Express Post network, which means Express items travelling between them come with Australia Post's next-business-day guarantee. Not every city gets that: Darwin is outside the guarantee, and Perth has it only from the CBD. The conditions that matter:

  • Lodge it Monday to Friday during Post Office opening hours. Yellow Express street boxes generally close about 4pm, but that varies by location.
  • Parcel Lockers and red street boxes both void the guarantee, even with Express packaging.
  • A late guaranteed parcel earns a refund of the postage, and that is the full extent of it.
  • Public holidays are not delivery days, so a holiday at the receiving end pushes the arrival back.

Whoever ends up with your card, get answers to these first:

  • What is the price range for this fault, and will you put it in writing?
  • Is there a written warranty, how many months does it run, and does it include labour?
  • Is the diagnostic fee waived if I proceed, and what is the bill if the fault proves unrepairable?

A Melbourne shop is the better choice when one near you does component work, states its terms up front and can take the card over the counter. You skip postage in both directions and the small risk any parcel carries. Posting to us makes more sense when you want published prices and a 12-month warranty confirmed before you spend anything, or when a local shop has already told you the card is finished and you want a second look.

Melbourne weather and what wears a card out

Melbourne summers are mild by Australian standards. Bureau of Meteorology mean maximum temperatures are 24.7°C in December, 26.8°C in January and 25.9°C in February. Among the other capitals outside New South Wales, only Hobart has a cooler summer on those figures.

That makes the weather an unlikely culprit on its own. What wears a card down is years of heating and cooling under load: thermal paste pumps out from under the cooler, pads dry and harden, and dust packs into the fins. The warning signs are a hotspot reading well above the core temperature, clocks dropping during long sessions, and fans that never settle. A thermal service fixes the lot, starting from $330, and it normally clears the bench in a day or two.

One fault that does show up in Melbourne search results has nothing to do with the weather: melted 12VHPWR and 12V-2x6 power connectors on high-end cards. A browned or misshapen plug is physical damage at the connector, sometimes reaching the traces behind it. Stop using the card and book it in for connector repair, which starts from $370. If the problem is coloured blocks or lines rather than heat, run through our GPU artefacting checks before posting, because a driver, cable or monitor fault can look the same.

Packing and posting from Melbourne

  1. Use the counter. Hand the parcel over at a Post Office on a weekday rather than using a red box or Parcel Locker. The guarantee stays intact and you walk out with proof of lodgement.
  2. Post early in the week. An Express parcel lodged on a Friday is due on Monday, so it spends the weekend in the network. Monday to Wednesday is better.
  3. The retail box stays behind. Aramex warns against boxes that announce electronics inside, and a graphics card box does exactly that. We need the card alone with its cooler fitted, so cables and brackets stay in Melbourne too.
  4. Double-box it. Anti-static bag, then a snug rigid box, then an outer box with at least 7cm of cushioning all round. Shake the parcel: any movement means it needs more padding. A "Fragile" sticker will not save a loose card.
  5. Check the return parcel before the cardboard goes out. On the way back you are the recipient, and damage claims are made by the recipient, with the card and packaging shown at a Post Office.

The full packing steps, including what to write on the note inside, are on the national mail-in page.

A newer card should go back to the shop first

If the card was bought new from a business and has not been misused, take it back to the retailer before posting it anywhere. Australian Consumer Law puts the claim with the seller, and the seller is not allowed to redirect you to the manufacturer. With a major failure, you are the one who picks refund or replacement. The consumer guarantees stand underneath any manufacturer's warranty, which cannot remove them, and they may still apply after the warranty period ends. Cards bought in a private sale are not covered, and that is where repair becomes the practical route.

Questions

Can a graphics card be repaired?

Often, yes. A dead or misbehaving card has usually lost one supporting component, such as a MOSFET in the power section, a GDDR memory package, a connector or the BIOS chip, and that component can be swapped. The bench diagnostic identifies it before anything is spent on parts. Some cards are beyond saving, and when that is the outcome you pay $150 plus return postage to Melbourne, along with an explanation of what failed.

How much does GPU repair cost in Melbourne?

The same as it costs in Sydney, because the card comes to the same bench. Repasting and firmware recovery begin from $330. A connector costs from $370, a power stage from $430, memory chip work from $530 and a reball from $630. The $150 diagnostic comes off the bill if you proceed. On top of that you pay postage in both directions, using whichever Australia Post service and level of cover you pick.

Can you fix a melted 12VHPWR connector?

Yes. Melted 12VHPWR and newer 12V-2x6 connectors fall under connector, port and trace repair, from $370. The damaged connector comes off, the pads and traces underneath are checked for heat damage, and a new connector goes on. If the heat has spread further into the board, the fixed quote tells you so before any work starts. A card young enough to still have manufacturer cover should go that way first.

How long does it take to post a graphics card from Melbourne to Sydney?

Australia Post estimates Express Post from Melbourne to Sydney Metro postcodes, which include ours, at one business day, and Parcel Post at two to three. Between those two capitals, Express has a next-business-day guarantee provided it goes over a Post Office counter, or into a yellow box, on a weekday before closing, never via a red box or Parcel Locker. Coming home takes about as long. All of these are estimates.

Why not use a Melbourne repair shop?

If a Melbourne shop does component-level work, publishes its prices and puts a warranty term in writing, using it saves postage and transit time, and that can be the better call. A large chain in the Melbourne results advertises board work without publishing prices or a warranty term. We list our prices, give 12 months on parts and labour, and waive the $150 diagnostic when you go ahead.
