What a Brisbane search turns up, and the questions worth asking

At the time of writing, the top result for graphics card repair in Brisbane is a Reddit thread, not a repairer. Under it: general computer shops, a national chain that shows no prices and no warranty term, and one shop whose mention of replacing diodes and capacitors runs to a few lines. We found no Brisbane business clearly presenting itself as a component-level graphics card specialist. A quiet website proves nothing either way, so ask anyone you are considering:

  • Will you replace the part that failed, or sell me a new card?
  • How much is the diagnostic, and does it come off the repair?
  • Do I get a fixed price before you start?
  • How many months does the repair warranty run?
  • If it cannot be fixed, what is the bill?

Ours, in the same order: the failed part is replaced; $150, taken off when you proceed; yes, a fixed quote; 12 months on the replaced parts, for the same fault; and the $150 plus return postage, with an explanation of the failure.

Three situations favour staying in Brisbane. A recent card goes to its retailer first (see below). If you are not yet sure the card is the culprit, a local shop can test it in another PC before any postage is spent, and the GPU artefacting guide lists the home tests worth running first. And when a card's resale value is only a couple of hundred dollars, a $150 diagnostic plus two postage legs rarely makes sense.

Queensland summers, storm season and the faults they cause

The Bureau of Meteorology's mean maximums for Brisbane are 29.7°C in December, 30.4°C in January and 30.2°C in February. A card cools itself with the air in the room, so a hot room leaves its cooler less margin, and over a few summers the paste and pads between the chips and the heatsink can dry out and stop moving heat well. What you notice: fans loud at light load, clock speeds sagging in games, crashes that seem to follow the weather. The remedy is a thermal service: from $330, typically one or two bench days.

Storm season is the other local pattern, and Brisbane City Council puts it at October to March. Energex says lightning is the most common cause of power surges and spikes, and it lists computers among the equipment commonly affected. If a surge gets past the power supply, it can damage the power stages that step the voltage down for the graphics chip. That kind of fault may show as fans spinning for a second before the card cuts out, or a PC that will not start with the card fitted. Power stage repair starts from $430.

Two cautions. A surge can hit more than one component, so rule the power supply and motherboard in or out before blaming the card. And the Queensland Government's advice costs nothing: it recommends unplugging electronic devices to protect them from power surges, which for a PC means pulling the plug when a storm is forecast.

Posting from Brisbane: practical points

  • Go to the counter. Red street boxes and Parcel Lockers void the Express guarantee, and the counter receipt doubles as proof of lodgement. In storm season, carry the box in something waterproof.
  • Consider Express on this route. One business day each way instead of two to four can have a thermal job back up to six business days sooner, and Brisbane–Sydney is covered by the next-day guarantee.
  • Double-box it. Australia Post's advice is a snug, rigid inner box inside an outer box, with cushioning between the two. Sendle suggests anti-static bagging, a minimum of 7cm of cushioning, and shaking the sealed box to check nothing shifts. Do not rely on a “Fragile” label.
  • Card alone, plain outer box. Cooler on; cables, bracket and retail box stay behind; job number inside. Aramex warns against boxes that advertise electronics.

The mail-in hub has the complete packing steps, water-cooled cards included.

Under warranty or recently bought: the retailer comes first

Where the card is fairly new, return it to the Brisbane shop or online retailer you bought it from before it goes in the post to anyone. Your consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law are separate from the manufacturer's warranty, cannot be taken away by it, and can outlast it. The claim runs through the seller, who is not allowed to point you to the manufacturer instead, and with a major failure you decide between refund and replacement. Misuse damage and private-sale purchases are not covered; for those, or after a refusal, repair by post is the next step.

Questions

Can a dead GPU be revived?

Often, yes. A card that shows no picture, will not let the PC start, or runs its fans and then stops has often lost just one part, such as a power stage or a memory chip, and the graphics processor itself may be untouched. Replacing that part brings the card back. Not every card can be saved, which is why the $150 diagnostic comes first. If yours is beyond repair, you pay that and the return postage, and we explain what failed.

My graphics card died in a storm. Can it be fixed?

Often it can. Energex names lightning as the most common cause of power surges and spikes, and a surge that gets past the power supply can damage the power stages feeding the graphics chip. Replacing them starts from $430. Before posting, try the card in another PC if you can, because the same surge may have hit the power supply or motherboard. Mention the storm on the form so the diagnostic starts in the right place.

How long does a graphics card last?

There is no set number of years; it depends on how hot and how hard the card runs, and how much dust its cooler collects. Brisbane's January mean maximum is 30.4°C, so a PC in an un-air-conditioned room works its cooler hard and the paste and pads can dry out sooner. When a card stops, the cause is often one component rather than the whole board, so failure need not be the end. A thermal service, from $330, is the cheapest way to extend its life.
